# Arena of the week: Belgrade Arena (Belgrade)

## Facts to verify
- Capacity: the EuroLeague API says **21,000**; Wikipedia and Wikidata say **18,386**. Cards and copy use the EuroLeague figure. The design brief asked for "at least 20,000" seats (Wikipedia).
- Opening: 31 July 2004 (Wikidata P1619 + Wikipedia: FIBA Diamond Ball). Wikipedia also says the first event was a 20 September 2000 rally in the unfinished building, and that it only got its permanent licence on 4 November 2006. We use 31 July 2004.
- The lost tournaments (1994 Basketball World Championship, 1999 World Table Tennis Championships), construction start 1992: Wikipedia only. Used in the caption and the X post ("12 years", 1992 to 2004).
- 22,198 = ABA League all-time record, Game 5 of the 2023 ABA Finals, 22 June 2023 (Wikipedia). The interior photo is titled "2022–23 ABA League, Game 5 Partizan - Crvena Zvezda" and dated 2023-06-22 on Commons, so it should be that very game. Slide 2.
- 24,232 = Zvezda vs Budivelnyk, Eurocup quarter-final, 26 March 2014, won 79–70 in OT; record for the Eurocup and for the arena (Wikipedia). Slide 4.
- Name history (Kombank Arena 2012, Štark Arena 2017, "Belgrade Arena" restored 3 April 2024): Wikipedia. Caption.
- Game time: Zvezda vs Maccabi, Tue 27 Oct 2026, 19:00 UTC = 21:00 Athens (EuroLeague API).

**Why this angle:** construction started in 1992, the city lost two world championships before the doors opened, and it still opened in 2004. Now it's home to both Belgrade giants, and on Tuesday Zvezda host Maccabi (then Bayern on Thursday).
