# Arena of the week: Palau Blaugrana (Barcelona)

## Facts to verify
- Capacity: **7,585** in the EuroLeague API, Wikidata and Wikipedia alike. Wikipedia: built for 5,696, remodelled in 1994 to today's 7,585. Caption.
- Opening: 23 October 1971 (Wikidata P1619); Wikipedia says "built in 1971".
- Name meaning "Blue and Garnet Palace" (Wikipedia). Slide 1 and caption.
- Same complex as the Camp Nou; home to FC Barcelona's basketball, handball, roller hockey and futsal teams (Wikipedia). Caption.
- 1992 Summer Olympics: roller hockey, taekwondo and judo (Wikipedia, "including", so maybe more). Slide 4 says "under this roof", not "on this floor": the main court was renovated in 2016.
- Retired basketball numbers: 4 Andrés Jiménez, 7 Nacho Solozábal, 11 Juan Carlos Navarro, 12 Roberto Dueñas, 15 Epi (Wikipedia). Caption names Navarro and Epi.
- Not used: Wikipedia's "Nou Palau Blaugrana" section says the Palau will be demolished under Espai Barça, with work "expected to start by the 2017–18 season". Clearly out of date, so left out.
- Interior photo (Commons "Dracs Palau Blaugrana Eurolliga 2010.jpg", 2010-03-23) shows the Dracs fan section at a EuroLeague game, with a banner "El Palau és un santuari del bàsquet". We don't name the opponent; subtitle says "A EuroLeague night at the Palau · March 2010".
- Exterior photo (Commons "Palau Blaugrana (Barcelona, ESP).jpg", CC BY 3.0) has no date on Commons.
- Game times: Barça vs Paris, Tue 17 Nov 2026, 19:30 UTC = 21:30 Athens; Barça vs Crvena Zvezda, Fri 20 Nov, 21:30 Athens (EuroLeague API). Fenerbahce visit on Tue 24 Nov too.

**Why this angle:** a 1971 dome named for the club's colours, an Olympic venue in 1992, and two EuroLeague home games this week.
